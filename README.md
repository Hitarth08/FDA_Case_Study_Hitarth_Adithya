# Harshad Mehta Securities Scam – Fraud Detection Case Study

## Overview
This project analyzes the **Harshad Mehta Securities Scam of 1992**, one of India's most significant financial frauds. The case study examines how banking and market loopholes were exploited, why existing controls failed, the impact of the fraud, and the reforms introduced afterward. FDA_Case_Study_Harshad_Mehta

## Key Areas Covered
- Background and context of the 1992 scam
- How the fraud was executed using:
  - Ready Forward (RF) deals
  - Fake Bank Receipts (BRs)
  - Misrouting of bank cheques
  - Stock price ramping
  - Rolling over funds
  - Collusion
- Control and regulatory failures
- Exposure and timeline of the scam
- Financial and market impact
- Legal action and recovery
- Reforms introduced after the scam
- Fraud detection and prevention lessons

## Fraud Detection Perspective
The case is analyzed from a **fraud detection and risk management perspective**, focusing on control weaknesses such as inadequate reconciliation, paper-based processes, weak supervision, excessive reliance on intermediaries, and lack of independent verification. FDA_Case_Study_Harshad_Mehta

The study also highlights how modern systems can use **electronic records, automated reconciliation, anomaly detection, transaction monitoring, and independent verification** to identify similar patterns earlier. FDA_Case_Study_Harshad_Mehta

## Major Lessons
1. Reconcile transactions with independent records.
2. Keep intermediaries away from direct control of funds.
3. Investigate unusual returns and abnormal market movements.
4. Use technology and automated monitoring to reduce manual loopholes.
5. Maintain strong regulatory and internal controls.
6. Reputation should never replace proper due diligence.

## Conclusion
The Harshad Mehta scam demonstrated how weaknesses across **processes, technology, controls, and supervision** can combine to enable large-scale financial fraud. The reforms that followed—including stronger SEBI powers, electronic trading, dematerialisation, and improved securities settlement—significantly strengthened India's financial infrastructure.
